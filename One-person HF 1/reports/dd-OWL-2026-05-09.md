# BLUE OWL CAPITAL (NYSE: OWL) — FUNDAMENTAL DEEP DIVE
**Research Date: May 9, 2026**

---

## ONE-LINER VERDICT
Structurally high-quality alt-manager — permanent capital moat, 15% AUM growth, 58% FRE margins — but trading at deep discount to peers after a securities class action lawsuit and a dividend that currently exceeds distributable earnings, creating a credibility and sustainability overhang that must clear before a re-rating occurs.

## 1. BUSINESS MODEL & REVENUE QUALITY

### What Does Blue Owl Do?
Blue Owl Capital is a pure-play permanent capital alternative asset manager with **$314.9B in AUM** as of March 31, 2026. It earns money primarily through management fees on long-duration or permanently locked capital. The business is organized into three platforms:

**Credit (Direct Lending):** $159.2B AUM (51% of total). Originates senior secured loans to sponsor-backed middle-market and upper-middle-market companies through OBDC and OBDC II. Fees earned on invested assets, not committed capital — stable and recurring.

**Real Assets:** $85.1B AUM (27% of total), up 27% YoY — fastest-growing platform. Includes net lease real estate (Oak Street), real estate credit, and digital infrastructure. The $2.4B Sila Realty Trust acquisition (announced April 2026, closing Q2-Q3 2026) adds a healthcare-focused triple-net-lease portfolio.

**GP Strategic Capital (Dyal):** $70.6B AUM (22% of total), up 5% YoY. Acquires minority equity stakes in established PE and hedge fund managers — royalty-like income tied to GP AUM growth. Most defensible, slowest-growing.

### Fee Structure — The Permanent Capital Advantage
- **Total AUM:** $314.9B | **Fee-Paying AUM (FPAUM):** $188.4B (59.8% of total)
- **AUM not yet paying fees:** $29.9B — represents ~$349M in annualized management fees once deployed
- **Permanent capital as % of management fees: ~85%**

This is the structural moat. Unlike Blackstone or KKR, ~85% of management fees come from vehicles with no fixed end date (BDCs, net lease REITs, semi-liquid credit). AUM does not cliff off at fund maturity.

## 4. EARNINGS QUALITY & KEY RISKS

### GAAP-to-DE Reconciliation Assessment
The gap is structurally legitimate (amortization, UP-C mechanics) but SBC ($673M in 2025 = 23.5% of revenue) is the most contested item — real dilution being added back. The 2026 SBC step-down to ~$365M is a meaningful improvement and will narrow the GAAP/DE gap.

### Key Risks
1. **Securities Class Action (Goldman v. Blue Owl Capital, SDNY):** Class period Feb-Nov 2025. Plaintiffs allege false/misleading statements about OBDC II redemption pressure and the OBDC/OBDC II merger. Names Co-CEOs Ostrover and Lipschultz. Settlement risk: $50M–$300M+ range. Reputational risk to private wealth fundraising channel is potentially greater than financial cost. **Active, unresolved.**

2. **Dividend Exceeds DE (121% payout ratio):** Any earnings miss triggers dividend-cut risk. The stock is yield-driven; a cut would likely produce a 15-25% selloff. Management has committed to $0.92 for 2026, but this requires 10%+ DE/share growth just to hit 100% coverage.

3. **Credit Cycle Risk at $159B Scale:** Direct lending has 12 bps gross loss rate today — pristine. But the portfolio is untested at this scale through a full credit cycle. Floating-rate loans benefit from high rates but would be impaired in a severe recession/credit crunch.

4. **AUM Growth Deceleration:** GP Strategic Capital grew only 5% YoY. If private wealth fundraising softens, overall AUM growth decelerates and the revenue pipeline narrows.

5. **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Lower rates compress net interest income on the direct lending book. Management acknowledged this headwind on the Q1 call.

6. **Key-Man Risk:** Founders Ostrover and Lipschultz are relationship-critical. No audit flags or going concern language in 2025 10-K.

## 5. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING

### Moat Assessment: **Narrow-to-Wide, Platform-Dependent**

- **Dyal/GP Stakes (Wide):** Proprietary network of PE manager relationships. Once a stake is held, the relationship is quasi-permanent and self-compounding as the underlying GP's AUM grows. Competition exists (Petershill/Goldman, Bonaccord) but the universe of quality GPs willing to sell stakes is limited — Blue Owl is a first-mover with 6 vintages closed.

- **Direct Lending (Narrow):** Scale and origination relationships create barriers, but the $1.5T direct lending market is competitive. Apollo and Ares are formidable. Blue Owl differentiates on upper-middle-market focus and speed of execution.

- **Net Lease Real Estate (Narrow):** Oak Street and the Sila acquisition create a meaningful platform, but cap rate compression and competition from REITs limit excess returns.

**Switching costs:** High in credit (loans stay in vehicles until maturity/payoff). Moderate in real assets. LP investors in BDCs are locked to market prices, not NAV redemptions — an advantage for AUM stability but a potential source of investor friction (as demonstrated by OBDC II).

**Customer/LP concentration:** Not specifically disclosed. Emphasis on institutional + growing private wealth channel. Private wealth brings higher retail risk (redemption pressure, as seen in OBDC II) but also broader distribution.

## 7. INVESTMENT THESIS

### Bull Case
1. **Permanent capital moat at distressed valuation.** At 9.5x EV/FRE vs. peers at 14-20x, the discount is extreme. If litigation resolves without material damage and DE grows into the dividend over 4-6 quarters, re-rating toward 13-14x FRE implies $14-18 fair value (+33-70%). The $29.9B AUM-not-yet-paying pipeline alone is ~$349M in incremental annual management fees — a built-in earnings engine.

2. **FRE margin expansion + SBC normalization.** FRE margins expanding (57.2% → 58.4%), SBC dropping $308M YoY ($673M → $365M guided), and revenue compounding at 13-15% — DE/share should grow faster than total FRE. The path to covering the $0.92 dividend is credible if Q2 and Q3 2026 continue the Q1 trajectory.

3. **8.7% yield with asymmetric option value.** The SpaceX co-investment (retained half at 10x money realized) is an example of upside optionality not in the DE model. Multiple similar equity co-investments across the credit book represent a call option on private market returns that the market is pricing at zero.

### Bear Case
1. **Dividend cut would be brutal and is not off the table.** The $0.92 dividend exceeds current DE by 21%. Stock is owned heavily by yield-seekers. Any adverse earnings development — a credit event in the direct lending book, slower fundraising, or a large litigation settlement — forces a cut. A cut from $0.92 to $0.70 would likely send the stock back to $7-8 given the multiple compression and sentiment damage.

2. **Securities class action is a live tail risk.** SDNY lawsuit (Goldman v. Blue Owl) alleging securities fraud. Settlement range is wide ($50M to $300M+) and outcome is uncertain. If the litigation produces evidence of systemic LP misrepresentation, private wealth fundraising — the key growth vector — could seize up. The OBDC II redemption-blocking is already a public mark against the firm's retail investor treatment.

3. **Credit cycle at $159B scale is untested.** The direct lending portfolio has never faced a severe credit cycle at this size. A 50-100 bps annualized loss rate (vs. current 12 bps) would generate $800M-$1.6B in annual credit losses — material relative to annual FRE of $1.6B. The portfolio is not marked to market daily, so stress would emerge gradually through non-accruals and realized losses over multiple quarters.

### VERDICT: HOLD / STARTER POSITION

**Rating:** HOLD — Starter Position
**Price Target:** $9.00 (bear) / $12.00 (base, +14%) / $17.00 (bull, +61%)
**Horizon:** 12-18 months

Blue Owl has genuinely high-quality, durable fee streams, a defensible moat in GP stakes, and a long-term AUM compounding engine that should work over time. The valuation is unambiguously cheap relative to peers (9.5x EV/FRE vs. 14-20x for Ares, Brookfield, Blackstone). However, two specific overhangs justify caution rather than aggressive sizing:

The dividend currently exceeds DE — every earnings report is an event risk until Q3-Q4 2026 demonstrates coverage convergence. The securities class action creates tail risk that cannot be quantified. Both must clear before a re-rating is likely.

**Key variable to monitor:** Q2 2026 DE/share vs. $0.23 dividend (does management get above $0.21+ quarterly run rate, signaling path to coverage?). Litigation settlement announcement (size and admission of wrongdoing, if any).

**What would change the view to BUY:** Litigation settled for <$100M with no admission of liability + two consecutive quarters of DE ≥ $0.22/share. At that point, the 9.5x EV/FRE multiple is unjustifiably cheap and a significant re-rating is likely.
